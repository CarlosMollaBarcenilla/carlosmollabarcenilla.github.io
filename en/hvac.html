Master's project, University of Liège

HVAC Simulation

I designed from scratch, and simulated hour by hour over a full year, the air conditioning system of a 200 m² office building in Palermo (Italy): air handling unit, four-pipe fan coils and a reversible heat pump. The goal was to bring the building close to nearly zero energy consumption without sacrificing thermal comfort.

Course “Energy Flexibility in Buildings”

  • −44 %annual cost of the optimised scenario
  • 13.65kWh/m² per year of final energy use

The challenge

A mild climate, but very humid and with demanding summers

Palermo has mild winters and hot summers: with the typical meteorological year (TMY), the outdoor temperature ranges from 2.1 °C to 33.5 °C, with an average relative humidity of 74.7 %. Dehumidification is therefore an essential part of the design.

Before sizing any equipment, I simulated the building without air conditioning. In summer the indoor temperature reached 45 °C, even above the outdoor temperature, and in winter it dropped to 9 °C. Most hours fell outside the acceptable thermal comfort categories, which confirmed the need for an active system in both seasons.

  • ComfortOperating range of 20 to 24 °C in occupied zones.
  • Air qualityHygienic ventilation of 3.6 m³/h per m² and occupancy according to ASHRAE 62.1.

Scenarios

Which measures really reduce the cost

With the complete system running all year as the base case, I evaluated different operating and envelope measures one by one. I then combined the most effective ones in a final scenario. Costs are annual, with an average electricity price in Italy of 0.35 €/kWh.

Analysis

Main results

01 / 07

Without air conditioning

Passive simulation of the building, with no active equipment, to understand its baseline thermal behaviour.

Indoors it reaches 45 °C in summer and drops to 9 °C in winter.

02 / 07

Baseline comfort

Category 4, the one with the greatest discomfort, dominates in every zone. Air conditioning was essential in both seasons.

Between 60 and 65 % of hours in category 4, depending on the zone.

03 / 07

Ventilation only

With the air handling unit and the hygienic air flow, humidity is under control, but the ventilation air is not enough to cover the sensible loads.

The maximum indoor temperature drops from 45 °C to 35 °C.

04 / 07

Complete system

Adding the fan coils covers the sensible loads in each zone and comfort is maintained almost every hour. This validated the 10 kW size of the heat pump.

Baseline consumption of 4,869 kWh of electricity and €1,704 per year.

05 / 07

Final scenario

By combining switch-off outside working hours, solar control and the heat pump, comfort during occupied hours stays within the acceptable categories.

2,729 kWh per year and a cost of €955: 44 % less than the base case.

06 / 07

Dehumidification

The air handling unit removes 1,878 litres of water per year. The demand is concentrated in summer, coinciding with the months of highest specific humidity.

Peak in August: 851 litres in the month and up to 53 litres in a single day.

07 / 07

Consumption breakdown

In the final scenario, the heat pump compressor accounts for most of the consumption and the fans for the rest.

Compressor 2,128 kWh (78 %) and fans 602 kWh (22 %).

From study to project

Regulations and BIM methodology

Applied in the study

Design criteria

Occupancy and ventilation according to ASHRAE 62.1, comfort assessed by thermal comfort categories, and an envelope based on the study by D’Agostino et al. (2019) for office buildings in the Mediterranean climate.

In a real project in Italy

Italian regulatory framework

The design should be checked against the Minimum Requirements Decree (DM 26/06/2015), with energy performance calculated according to UNI/TS 11300, together with UNI EN 16798-1 for ventilation and indoor environment.

Equivalent in Spain

RITE and CTE

In Spain, the same installation would be governed by the Regulation on Thermal Installations in Buildings (RITE) and by the Basic Energy Saving Document of the Technical Building Code (CTE DB-HE).

Next step

MEP installations in BIM

The natural next step would be to transfer this installation to a BIM model to coordinate ducts, pipes and equipment with the architecture and link the model to the energy simulation. It is the methodology I want to train in next.

Conclusions

What I learned from the building

  • The heat pump is essential: with electric resistance heaters the annual cost would be 3.6 times higher.
  • The occupancy-based operation is the most cost-effective measure: switching the system off outside working hours cuts the cost by 30 % without losing comfort during occupied hours.
  • In Palermo, solar control matters more than insulation: external shading reduces the cooling demand and the peak power, while going from 4 to 2 cm of insulation barely changes the cost.
  • Combining these measures, the building reaches 13.65 kWh/m² per year, in the same order of magnitude as very high efficiency buildings.

This is an idealised scenario: it assumes correct and consistent use of the solar shading and does not include distribution losses in pipes and ducts or the consumption of pumps and valves, which usually add between 10 and 15 % to the actual consumption.
