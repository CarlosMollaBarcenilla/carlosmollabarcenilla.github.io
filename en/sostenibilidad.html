Sustainability

During my studies I have come to realise that the planet's resources are finite and that the climate is already changing. The decisions made this decade about how we produce energy, materials and buildings will shape the quality of life of future generations. Sustainability is no longer an option: it is a condition for industry to remain viable. That is why I have always focused my studies on helping to drive the energy transition. As examples, I present a project on how to reduce the CO₂ equivalent of cements through different processes and scenarios, and my Bachelor's thesis on CO₂ capture by adsorption.

Project 01

Life cycle of cement

Master's project, University of Liège

In the course Life Cycle Assessment and Ecodesign I worked in a team to model cement production in Spain with SimaPro, based on the study by García-Gusano et al. (2015): first clinker, then five types of cement and, finally, a scenario with alternative fuels. My model reproduces the reference result for climate change with a difference of 0.3 %.

  • StandardISO 14040 / 14044
  • ToolsSimaPro 10.2 and Ecoinvent 3, ILCD 2011 method
  • ScopeCradle to gate
  • Functional units1 t of clinker and 1 t of cement
Why it matters

Industry has to decarbonise too

The energy transition is usually told from the point of view of power generation, but a huge share of emissions comes from what we manufacture. Cement is the clearest example: it is essential for any infrastructure and, at the same time, one of the materials with the largest carbon footprint on the planet.

What struck me most when studying it is that more than half of its CO₂ does not come from burning fuel, but from the chemistry of the process itself. Switching fuels is not enough: the product's recipe has to change too. And to know which change works, you have to measure it.

Results

Less clinker, less impact

I compared five cements with different proportions of clinker and of the materials that replace it: blast furnace slag, pozzolana, fly ash and limestone. Portland cement (CEM I) has the highest impact in every category and serves as the reference.

The pattern is clear: the less clinker, the lower the impact. Composite cement (CEM V/A), with only 52 % clinker, reduces impacts by between 30 and 45 % and meets the same long-term strength standards. Select a category to compare.

Improvement scenario

Not every alternative is cleaner

Replacing half of the fossil fuel with used tyres reduces toxicity, photochemical ozone and acidification, but increases climate change by around 10 %: synthetic rubber is also of fossil origin. Without a life cycle assessment, this measure would have been presented as an improvement.

Base scenario50 % tyres
Proposal

Combine the tyres with biomass, such as wood waste, to maintain the calorific value while reducing the carbon footprint.

Conclusions

Lessons for industry

  • The production of clinker accounts for most of the environmental burden of cement, and climate change is the critical impact category.
  • Reducing the clinker content is the most effective measure: composite cement reduces impacts by between 30 and 45 % compared with Portland.
  • Fuel substitution requires a data-driven selection: only biomass fractions guarantee a real reduction in CO₂ emissions.
  • The reliability of a life cycle assessment depends on the quality of the inventory, so its critical review is an essential step.

Project 02

CO₂ capture by adsorption

Bachelor's thesis, I3A, University of Zaragoza

As part of a project at the Aragon Institute of Engineering Research (I3A), I developed Python tools to speed up the design of adsorption columns, which capture the CO₂ from gas streams such as biogas. The key question is how long a column takes to become saturated and let the CO₂ escape: the so-called breakthrough point.

Answering it in the laboratory requires many tests. My work was to build a numerical workflow that predicts it from the operating conditions, to save experimental time and adsorbent material.

  1. 01FAIR data

    Tools built with pyGAPS to analyse adsorption isotherms and convert them to the NIST database format.

  2. 02136 simulations

    Latin hypercube design of experiments and a script that automates the RUPTURA simulator from Excel.

  3. 03Surrogate model

    Gaussian process regression with scikit-learn to predict the breakthrough point under any conditions.

  4. 04Simple equations

    Tensor decomposition (97.6 % accuracy) and symbolic regression with PySR to obtain formulas usable in the laboratory.
